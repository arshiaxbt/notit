# Engine notes: decisions, ambiguities, findings

Written against `docs/SPEC.md` v2 plus two announced changes: §4.1 step 3b (`paid` is reported net of It's own cut) and §5.5 (`storage_unregister` runs `settle(now)` first). Where the spec was silent or could be read two ways, the engine takes the most literal reading; each such case is listed here so the contract can be checked against the same choice.

## 1. Ambiguities and how the engine resolves them

0. **Step 3b can go negative: the engine clamps it at 0.** `paid = pot − (floor(eff(it) × index_before / RAY) − floor(eff(it) × index_after / RAY))`. The four floors are independent, so when everybody except It holds less than about one unit in total (for example one dust share left behind by a ceil-rounded transfer of a whole balance), It's own cut can come out one unit larger than the pot. The engine uses `max(0, …)`. A contract that subtracts unchecked u128 values would panic inside `settle`, and since every state-changing call settles first, it would be stuck for good. The case is reachable with ordinary calls; `test/engine.test.ts` ("net paid is clamped at 0…") and the `net-paid-clamp` scenario both reproduce it.
1. **`storage_unregister` with a balance and no `force`.** §5.5 says it "requires `eff == 0` unless `force`", but §10 has no error code for it. `unregister(account, false, now)` returns `false` and writes nothing, not even the settlement (the contract panics, which reverts it). It also returns `false` and writes nothing for an account that is not registered; a contract that returns `false` there without panicking would keep its settlement. Views are identical either way, and the scenarios never unregister an unregistered account.
2. **`snapshot()` hero entries.** §8 gives the key order and `{ id, shares, scale, claimed }` for accounts, but only "heroes (array sorted by id)". The engine writes `{ id, saves, burned, collected }`. `stats` uses the §6 order: `rounds_saved, rounds_collapsed, current_streak, longest_streak, closest_call_ms, total_burned, total_paid`. History entries use the §7 JSON. Sorting is by UTF-16 code unit, which equals byte order for NEAR account ids.
3. **`snapshot()` cannot show everything.** `claimed` survives unregistration, but the snapshot only lists registered accounts, so a claimed-then-unregistered account is invisible to a differential test. The config and `genesis_ns` are not in the snapshot either (not listed in §8).
4. **`rounds_closed` event.** `to_round` is not defined. The engine uses `from_round` = stored `round` before the settle and `to_round` = `round` after it (the new current round, after any dormant jump). `data` is an object, as written in §7, not a one-element array. `it` is the stored It while that settle ran (null if none), `paid` the sum of the pots it closed.
5. **`ft_burn` amount on a forced unregister.** Not specified. The engine emits `floor(eff × index / RAY)` after the settlement, and only when `eff > 0`.
6. **`heroes()` has no `now` in §8** although `get_heroes` is a view. `heroes()` returns stored values; `heroes(now)` adds the collapses that would be settled right now to It's `collected`, matching what `account(id, now)` reports.
7. **`armed` in a saved round.** §3 defines `armed` without reference to `round_hero`, so `state().armed` stays `true` in a round that is already saved (where "an unsaved close" cannot happen).
8. **`now` before genesis, or going backwards.** `current_round` saturates at 0. If a caller passes a `now` earlier than the current round's start, nothing is settled and `elapsed` is clamped to 0. The contract can never see this.
9. **Bad config values.** Besides the §2 rules, non-integer or out-of-range numbers (not u32 / u64 / u128) also throw `ERR_PARAMS`.
10. **Overflow.** BigInt never overflows; the engine does not emulate u128/u256 panics. With `claim_amount × claim_cap ≤ 10^32` none should be reachable.

## 2. Things in the spec worth a second look

These are not engine bugs; they are properties of the rules that surprised me while testing.

- **`paid` / `collected` / `total_paid` are net of It's own cut** (step 3b). The pot that is minted to It is still 15 % of the whole supply, It's own balance included; before step 3b the stats counted that gross figure, and experiment C reported 465,802 NOTIT "paid" on a 40,000 supply where 30,278 actually moved. The net figure equals It's balance gain up to 1 unit per collapse.
- **The 8-round cap can be reset by It.** `collapse_run` is reset by any volunteer, including It. §11 says "It never gains from volunteering", but an It that volunteers in the final block (0.2 % of supply) re-arms the game for another 8 charged rounds. Against a passive crowd that is strongly profitable: 8 collapses move 1 − 0.85^8 = 72.7 % of everyone else's balance. The cap only protects holders from an It that has gone away. Experiment C shows it: once one sniper is the only solvent volunteer, it stays It and collects on every missed round.
- **Cold start.** Before the first volunteer nothing is charged, so a holder who only weighs the loss avoided (§11 "myopic") never volunteers and the game never starts. The same holds after 8 collapses: the game stays dormant unless somebody values being It. Experiment B, cold start: `rationalTimer` saves 0 % of rounds, `throneAware` 99.8 %.
- **Lowest reachable cost rate is `min_cost_ppm + 1`.** `elapsed < round_ns`, so `floor((max − min) × elapsed / round_ns) ≤ max − min − 1`. With FAST the last nanosecond costs 2,001 ppm, not 2,000.
- **An It that wants to leave quietly cannot.** `storage_unregister` settles first, so rounds that already ended unsaved are charged and paid to It before it goes. An It with no shares of its own therefore needs `force` whenever something is pending, and the pots it was just paid are burned with it.
- **Rounding.** A collapse loses at most 1 unit of supply (the minted pot is floored). Balances sum to at most `total_supply`, short by up to one unit per account, and each account can lose up to one more share in a rescale. `transfer` uses a ceil, so the sender pays up to 1 unit more than `amount` once `index < RAY`. All tests assert these bounds.
- **`node --test test/`** (the script named in the brief) does not work on Node 24: a directory argument is loaded as a module and fails. The script is `node --test "test/*.test.ts"`.

## 3. Engine design decisions

- **Failed actions write nothing.** A contract panic reverts the settlement that ran at the start of the call. Each action therefore computes the settlement as a pure function, checks every requirement against that result, and only then commits it with its own writes. `snapshot()` after a failed call equals `snapshot()` before it (tested).
- **Views use the same function without committing**, so "views equal the state after `poke()`" holds by construction, including It's pending pot, its `collected`, a pending rescale and the trailing history entry (tested).
- **Order of checks** follows the order of the sentences in §5: `volunteer` raises `ERR_ALREADY_SAVED`, `ERR_NOTHING_TO_SAVE`, `ERR_WRONG_ROUND`, `ERR_COST_TOO_HIGH`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT`; `transfer` raises `ERR_ZERO_AMOUNT`, `ERR_SELF_TRANSFER`, `ERR_UNREGISTERED`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT`; `claim` raises `ERR_ALREADY_CLAIMED` before `ERR_CLAIMS_CLOSED`.
- **Registration is free**, so `ERR_STORAGE` is never raised. `register` does not settle and uses the stored `scale`; `unregister` settles first like every other action.
- **`ft_transfer_call` is not implemented** (§5.3 marks it contract-only).
- **Types.** u128/u256 and nanosecond timestamps are `bigint`; rounds, scales, counters and milliseconds are `number`. `volunteer` returns `{ cost, msLeft }`.
- **Extras beyond §8**: `config`, an optional `onEvent` sink with the §7 events in their JSON shape, `heroes(now?)`, and the pure helpers `costRatePpm`, `costFromRate`, `effectiveShares`, `validateConfig`.
- **Tooling.** `typescript` is the only dev dependency. `types/node-shim.d.ts` declares the handful of Node built-ins used by `src/lab.ts` and the tests, in place of `@types/node`. `tsconfig.json` sets `erasableSyntaxOnly`.

## 4. Simulator

- **Time.** A round has `slotsPerRound` decision points (default 20). Slot `s` is at `floor((round_ns − 1) × s / (slots − 1))`: slot 0 is the round opening (max cost), the last slot is the final nanosecond (2,001 ppm).
- **Order.** In every slot the agents are shuffled (Fisher–Yates, mulberry32) and asked in that order. The first `volunteer()` that succeeds wins and the rest of the round is skipped. A call rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT` is counted (`rejected`) and the slot goes on.
- **A `miss`** (sniper) is a transaction that lands after the deadline. The simulator drops it and counts it. On the contract it fails with `ERR_WRONG_ROUND` because of the `round` guard, which the simulator always passes.
- **World setup.** Engine round 0 can never be charged. In it, one faucet account claims the whole starting supply (`claim_amount` = the sum, `claim_cap` = 1) and transfers each agent its balance; at `index = RAY` that is exact and the faucet ends at zero. With `initialIt`, that agent volunteers in the final slot of engine round 1 and the measured rounds start in round 2; `balanceStart` and `supplyStart` are taken after that.
- **Outcomes** are read from the engine after each round is closed: `saved`, `collapsed` (cut paid to It) or `dormant` (nobody volunteered, nothing charged). `failureRate = (collapsed + dormant) / rounds`.
- **`recentFailureRate`** is the share of the last `failureWindow` (default 20) completed rounds that nobody saved, collapsed or dormant; 0 before any round has completed.
- **Strategies** (§11 payoffs; amounts as floating point):
  - `mixedFinalBlock`: armed round, not It, final slot only: volunteers with the first-pays equilibrium probability `firstPaysEquilibrium(n, q).p`, `q = cost_final / (cut × own_balance)`, `n` = holders with a positive balance other than It (see §5 A).
  - `rationalTimer(patience)`: armed round, not It: volunteers at the first slot where `cost_now < cut × own_balance × patience`.
  - `throneAware(patience)`: not It: volunteers at the first slot where `cost_now < (loss_avoided + throne) × patience`. `loss_avoided = cut × own_balance` if armed, else 0. `throne = cut × (supply − own_balance) × Σ_{k=1..8} f^k × (1 − cut)^(k−1)` with `f = recentFailureRate`: the k-th unsaved round in a row pays It the cut on everybody else's remaining balance, rounds are treated as failing independently with probability `f`, and at most 8 are charged. It is an estimate, not an equilibrium.
  - `altruist(h)`, `sniper(missRate)`, `freeRider` ignore prices and roles; in particular a sniper or altruist who is It keeps volunteering.

## 5. Lab experiments

- **A. Bystander effect.** For each N there are N players with 1,000 NOTIT each, all `mixedFinalBlock`, plus one extra account that also started with 1,000 NOTIT, became It in the setup round and never volunteers again. Every trial is one armed round on a fresh engine (`resetEachRound`), so the players are exactly equal in every trial. `q = c / (cut × x)` uses the players' actual balance `x` and the actual final-slot cost `c` read from the engine.
  - **Which equilibrium.** The textbook volunteer's dilemma (`p = 1 − q^(1/(n−1))`, `P(fail) = q^(n/(n−1))`, which SPEC §11 prints beside the first-pays condition) assumes every volunteer pays. Under the deployed rule only the first volunteer pays; later calls fail with `ERR_ALREADY_SAVED` and burn nothing. A submitter therefore pays with probability `E[1/(K+1)]`, `K ~ Binomial(n−1, p)`, which is `(1 − (1−p)^n) / (n·p)`, and the indifference condition is `q × (1 − (1−p)^n) / (n·p) = (1−p)^(n−1)`, with `P(fail) = (1−p)^n`. `firstPaysEquilibrium(n, q)` solves it; `classicDilemma(n, q)` keeps the textbook formula for the comparison column. Holders volunteer more readily than in the textbook case: at 10 players the round fails 3.6 % of the time, not 11.8 %.
  - **Uniqueness and the solver.** Rewritten as `h(p) = n / Σ_{k<n} (1−p)^(−k) = q`, the left side falls strictly from 1 to 0 on (0, 1), so for `0 < q < 1` and `n ≥ 2` the symmetric equilibrium is unique and bisection is safe. `q ≥ 1` gives `p = 0` (nobody volunteers; N = 75 and 100 in the table). A lone holder (`n = 1`) always pays when it submits and volunteers for sure when `q < 1`. `h` is evaluated with `log1p` / `expm1`, since `1 − (1−p)^n` loses all precision when `q` is close to 1 and `p` is tiny. The symmetric equilibrium is not the only one: there are also `n` asymmetric equilibria in which one designated holder always volunteers and nobody else does (the round never fails). The table is about the symmetric one.
  - **What the agreement shows.** The simulated players draw from the equilibrium probability, so "simulated ≈ equilibrium" checks the arithmetic, the random order and the engine. It says nothing about whether people behave this way.
  - `mixedEquilibriumP(q, n)` and `mixedEquilibriumFailure(q, n)` now return the first-pays values (they returned the textbook ones before); `BystanderRow.p` and `.theory` likewise, next to the explicit `equilibriumP`, `equilibriumFail`, `classicP`, `classicFail`.
- **B. Who pays.** 50 holders with Pareto(100 NOTIT, α = 1.16) balances floored to whole tokens. One run per strategy, same balances and same seed. Both runs start armed: the largest holder volunteers in a setup round. "Share of all burns" is in tokens burned; the top 5 are ranked by starting balance. The two columns are identical: with these holders somebody always holds more than `min_cost / cut` = 1.33 % of the supply, no round ever fails, the observed failure rate is 0 and the throne is worth nothing. The cold-start lines below the table are the same two strategies without the setup round, where they do differ.
- **B2. A dispersed crowd.** 100 holders with 1,000 NOTIT each (1 % of the supply, under the 1.33 % threshold). The last holder (`holder-100`) volunteers in a setup round; then 500 rounds per strategy with the same seed. "Top 5" are the first five holders by id, since all balances are equal. Myopic holders never volunteer: It collects 8 rounds and the game sleeps for the other 492. Throne-aware holders contest the throne and save 99 % of rounds, but burn 88 % of the supply doing it.
- **C. The idle get taxed.** 4 altruists (h = 0.3), 6 snipers (missRate 0.2) and 30 free riders with 1,000 NOTIT each, from a cold start.
- **D. Forward-looking holders.** If being It is worth `W` when a round opens and a round goes unsaved with chance `π`, then `W = π × (cut × S + W)`, so `W = cut × S × π / (1 − π)`. Taking the throne in the final block costs `min_cost × S`, and a transaction that loses the race costs nothing, so free entry forces `W ≤ min_cost × S`, hence `π ≤ min_cost / (cut + min_cost)` = 0.2 / 15.2 = 1.32 % with the deployed parameters. It is a bound for risk-neutral, equally fast, non-colluding holders; it ignores the 8-round cap and It's own share of the supply. The table puts the unsaved rate of four `throneAware` crowds of equal holders next to it (same set-up and seed as B2; the 100-holder row is B2's `throneAware` column). 20, 50 and 100 holders stay under the bound. 200 holders do not (4.0 %): `throneAware` is a rule of thumb that prices the throne from the failures of the last 20 rounds, so in a crowd where nobody is large enough to save myopically it forgets and lets a round fail every 20-odd rounds. That is a property of the heuristic, not a counterexample to the bound.

`npm run lab` prints these and writes the same numbers to `out/lab.json` (bigints as decimal strings in base units).

## 6. Differential-test scenarios

`npm run scenarios -w engine` writes `out/scenarios.json` (`src/scenarios.ts`; seeded, byte-identical across runs). The replay protocol is at the top of that file and implemented by `applyStep` / `replayScenarios`: run the call at block time `now`, compare its error code with `error`, call `poke()` at the same `now` whatever happened, then compare the stored state with `snapshot` if the step has one.

- **Size against step count.** The request was about 40 scenarios × 120 steps, a snapshot on every 8th step, and a file under about 700 KB. Those do not fit together: the steps alone are about 520 KB, and each snapshot carries the whole history, so the full set is 2.2 MB. The default keeps the 700 KB limit and the snapshot rule and shortens the scenarios: 34 mixed × 48 steps, 6 rescale (two of 96 steps, four of 56) and two targeted scenarios — 42 scenarios, 2,075 steps, 264 snapshots, 687 KB. `node src/scenarios.ts --full` writes the 40 × 120 set to `out/scenarios-full.json` (2.2 MB).
- **Mixed scenarios** draw calls at random with wrong `round` guards, too-low `max_cost`, zero / self / over-balance transfers, transfers to unregistered accounts, volunteers by poor or unregistered accounts and by the current It. Time moves by 0, by part of a round, to the last nanosecond or the first of a round, by 1–3 rounds, or by 9–40 rounds.
- **Rescale scenarios** volunteer in the last nanosecond of a round and jump 10 rounds, over and over. All six pass 128 collapses (`scale` 1); the first reaches `scale` 2, so some accounts are two rescales behind when they are next written.
- **`net-paid-clamp`** is the rounding case of note 0 above, with an extra snapshot right after the clamped collapse.
- **`it-unregisters`** has It leaving three times, with a snapshot right after each: empty with nothing pending (no force), with its balance and two rounds pending, and with no shares of its own and four rounds pending (the pots are minted, then burned).
- **`unregister`** is called with the step's `now` and settles first. It is only generated where it must succeed: on a registered account, forced, or unforced when its effective shares after that settlement are zero. It is followed by `claim` (expects `ERR_ALREADY_CLAIMED`, before and after re-registering) and `register`. `register` is `storage_deposit` and is never an error, also for an account that is already registered. Unregistering an account that is not registered is never generated.
- Every code of §10 is expected somewhere except `ERR_PARAMS`, `ERR_STORAGE` and `ERR_CLAIMS_CLOSED` (the cap of the fixed config is 100,000).
